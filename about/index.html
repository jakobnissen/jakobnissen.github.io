About

Hello, there! I'm Jakob. This blog is about programming, mostly, and sometimes biology.

How I got here

When studying in university to become a molecular biologist, I discovered my love for programming during a course in bioinformatics. I became obsessed, and one year later began a PhD in bioinformatics where I could combine my two loves: Genomics and coding.

Even though it was as late as 2016, Python had just replaced Perl as the lingua franca in bioinfo. I quickly developed a dislike for Perl and R, and dove headfirst into Python, wanting to learn as much as possible, and wanting to use Python for everything.

While Python was great and flexible, I spent the next year butting my head against the performance wall. At that time, I worked in metagenomics, a sub-field of bioinfo characterised by large datasets. When developing my main PhD project, Vamb, it quickly became clear that "Python for everything" just was not feasible. I really tried though - attempting to master Numpy, Numba, PyTorch and Cython, all in order to squeeze acceptable performance out of Python. Eventually, Vamb ended up using a combination of Numpy, PyTorch and Cython (yes, all three).

In my desperate search for good solutions, I stumbled upon Julia. Boy, could it run fast. After seeing my own clumsy Julia code easily beat my optimized Cython, I tried to give Julia a shot. Eventually, the weirdness wore off and its underlying design beauty began to shine through to me.

Much later, perhaps around early of 2020, I decided I needed to learn a statically compiled language to broaden my skills. Rust would complement Julia well, as the two languages had wildly different design goals. So I gave Rust a go, and had to quit in frustration. Tried again later, and failed again, but somehow it stuck the third time. Unlike Julia, I instantly liked Rust, even when I felt too dumb for it. It was just too well designed to not like.

Currently, I use all three languages on a daily basis at work, making scientific software.

Software

Besides this blog, I have made a few things, including:

Python

  • Vamb - variational autoencoders for metagenomic binning

Rust

  • alen - simple terminal alignment viewer.

  • samstrip - strip data from SAM files not needed for metagenomic binning

Julia

  • BinBencher.jl - metagenomic binning benchmarking and analysis software

  • BufferIO.jl - lower level, performant IO abstractions in Julia

  • MemoryViews.jl - low-level view into Memory in Julia

  • BGZFLib.jl - read and write BGZF files.

  • Automa.jl - regex to Julia compiler and parser generator

  • LibDeflate.jl - bindings to the fast de/compression library libdeflate.

  • BioSequences.jl - The BioJulia library for DNA, RNA and peptide sequences

  • BioSymbols.jl - The core DNA, RNA and amino acid type in Julia

  • Kmers.jl - optimized, static, integer-backed biological sequences

  • FASTX.jl - FASTA and FASTQ parsing in Julia

  • StackCollections.jl - optimized, fixed-bit collections.

  • PairwiseMappingFormat.jl PAF file parser

  • CIGARStrings.jl - Read and write CIGARs in Julia

  • XAMAuxData.jl - read and write SAM, BAM, PAF and GFA auxiliary data

  • ErrorTypes.jl - Rust-like recoverable error types.

  • MinHash.jl - efficient, generic minhashing.
